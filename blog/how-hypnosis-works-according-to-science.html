How Hypnosis Works According To Science

Shawn Huber · June 10, 2022

Hypnosis is a state of highly focused attention, and it's a state that you can learn to achieve. Despite common misconceptions, hypnosis isn't about losing control or being manipulated—it's actually a scientifically-backed method for accessing and reprogramming your subconscious mind.

The science behind hypnosis reveals how this powerful tool can unlock mental blocks, facilitate lasting change, and help you overcome challenges that conscious willpower alone can't address. Let's explore exactly how hypnosis works according to modern research and why it's becoming an increasingly popular therapeutic approach.

Unlocking Your Subconscious Mind

When you're hypnotized, your conscious mind steps back and allows your subconscious mind to take over. The subconscious mind makes up 90% of your brain, so it has significantly more power than the conscious mind. It controls what we do on a daily basis: our habits, emotions, and beliefs.

The conscious mind can only process so much information at once, and hypnosis helps us reach the parts of our brain that store our memories and emotions. This is where the real work happens—where limiting beliefs are stored and where lasting change can occur.

Key Understanding

The subconscious mind doesn't listen to reason or logic; it only listens to suggestion and repetition.

The Neuroplasticity Connection

Hypnosis facilitates neuroplasticity—the brain's ability to reorganize and form new neural connections. When we have new experiences, those experiences are encoded into our brains through this remarkable process.

Neuroplasticity can be compared to a tree growing more branches when it encounters wind or rain: it grows new neural pathways to accommodate new information. Hypnosis creates the optimal mental state for this rewiring to occur, allowing you to literally change your brain's programming.

How Hypnotic Suggestion Creates Change

The subconscious mind operates differently than our logical, conscious mind. If someone tells you something often enough, eventually you'll believe it just because they said it so many times. This is why advertisers use slogans like "Just do it" or "I'm lovin' it"—they want you to repeat their message until it becomes part of your belief system.

Hypnosis works very similarly—by repeating positive suggestions over and over again until they become part of your subconscious belief system. When this happens, you start acting differently without even realizing it.

When positive suggestions become part of your subconscious belief system, you start acting differently without even realizing it. — The Power of Subconscious Programming

Real-World Success Story

Here's a powerful example of how hypnosis works in practice: I had a client who suffered from chronic insomnia for 6 years. She had tried many different methods, from supplements to sleep studies, with no lasting success.

She originally came to see me for a physical injury. By allowing herself to relax deeply to overcome the injury, she also experienced the best sleep she'd had in six years. It's fascinating how interconnected our systems are—her subconscious worked on everything to make it all function better together.

The guidance of hypnosis helped her release both physical and mental stress, demonstrating how this approach addresses the whole person, not just isolated symptoms.

Modern Applications of Hypnotherapy

The most common use of hypnosis today is as a form of therapy. Hypnotherapy is an increasingly popular method for treating a range of psychological and behavioral challenges.

Common Uses Include:

  • Anxiety and stress management
  • Depression support
  • Smoking cessation
  • Weight management
  • Chronic pain relief
  • Sleep disorders
  • Performance enhancement

In psychology, hypnosis is defined as an altered state of consciousness that facilitates heightened focus and increased suggestibility. It's used as part of psychotherapy and can even be used for pain relief or to induce anesthesia during medical procedures (hypnoanesthesia).

Key Takeaways

  • Hypnosis accesses the powerful subconscious mind, which controls 90% of your daily behaviors and beliefs.
  • The process facilitates neuroplasticity, allowing your brain to form new, healthier neural pathways.
  • Repetitive positive suggestions during hypnosis become integrated into your belief system, creating automatic behavioral changes.
  • Modern hypnotherapy effectively treats anxiety, stress, habits, and various psychological challenges through this science-backed approach.
